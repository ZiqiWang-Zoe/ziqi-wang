  1. [TechCrunch]Nvidia puts $1.5B into SB Energy and up to $105B of credit behind OpenAI's Ohio campus(08-17)

    Nvidia will invest $1.5 billion in SoftBank- and OpenAI-backed developer SB Energy and extend up to $105 billion in credit for OpenAI's Ports-Pike data center near Cincinnati, locking itself in as the site's sole compute supplier. SEC filings put the campus at 4.25GW scaling to 8GW on Department of Energy land, fed by a $33 billion, 9.2GW on-site gas plant.

    Also: DIGITIMES (08-17)
  2. [Tom's Hardware]DDR5 is up nearly 500% in a year — a 128GB kit now costs $3,399(08-17)

    High-capacity DDR5 has risen close to 500% year over year, with a 64GB kit going from under $200 to over $1,100 and DDR4 dragged up 120–180% by the spillover. Hyperscalers have reportedly pre-paid for nearly all 2027 DRAM output, and SK hynix's CEO calls 2027 the worst supply year in the industry's history.

    Also: TrendForce (08-17)
  3. [Tom's Hardware]PJM asks FERC to black out new data centers before households(08-17)

    PJM would curtail new 50MW-plus sites that connect without bringing their own generation ahead of paying other customers to cut load, enforced through a new Large Load Registry, after two capacity auctions failed to secure enough supply. Its market monitor blames data centers for a 75.5% jump in regional power costs.

    Also: SemiAnalysis (08-16)
  4. [TrendForce]Samsung and SK hynix lift 1H26 fab spending 35% to KRW 43.2T, both at full utilization(08-17)

    Samsung put KRW 25.6 trillion into its Device Solutions division (+23.5%) and SK hynix KRW 17.6 trillion (+56.4%) into HBM, high-capacity DRAM and enterprise SSDs, with R&D up 51.5% and 98.4% respectively. Nvidia meanwhile fell to 13.35% of SK hynix revenue from 24%, and is absent from Samsung's top five customers — where Alphabet, Amazon and Apple now sit.

    Also: DIGITIMES (08-17)
  5. [TechCrunch]Groq raises $350M at a $3.5B valuation to finish its pivot from chipmaker to neocloud(08-17)

    Disruptive led the round with planned Nvidia participation, at half the $6.9 billion valuation Groq carried before Nvidia hired founder Jonathan Ross in a licensing deal. The one-time LPU challenger now runs 13 Nvidia-based data centers and plans to scale from 54MW to more than 200MW by 2027 — an Nvidia rival turned Nvidia customer.

  6. [TechCrunch]Stripe to buy AI gateway OpenRouter for more than $7B(08-16)

    Bloomberg reports the deal is done at north of $7 billion, roughly five times the $1.3 billion valuation OpenRouter held at its $113 million Series B in May. It routes across 400-plus models for 8 million users, putting Stripe at the metering layer for AI model consumption.

  7. [TrendForce]TSMC Arizona becomes its most profitable overseas fab, profit up 663%(08-17)

    Arizona posted a record NT$36.07 billion profit in 1H26 — more than 60% of TSMC's overseas subsidiary earnings — overtaking Nanjing, though Q2 slipped 8.2% sequentially as depreciation bit. With US investment now planned at $265 billion across 10 fabs, TSMC guides the overseas ramp to dilute gross margin by 3–4 points as the sites mature.

    Also: DIGITIMES (08-17)
  8. [Tom's Hardware]Optical interconnect to grow ten-fold to $144B by 2030 as copper hits its limit(08-17)

    A China Insights Consultancy forecast takes the datacenter optical interconnect market from $13.7 billion in 2024 to $144.4 billion in 2030, with silicon photonics claiming 63.7% of revenue and 1.6T plus 3.2T links accounting for roughly $110 billion of it. More than $15 billion has already moved into co-packaged optics and photonics in the past year, including Nvidia's $2 billion stakes in Coherent and Lumentum.

    Also: DIGITIMES (08-17)
  9. [DIGITIMES]AMD is reportedly eyeing Taalas, whose chips would skip HBM and advanced packaging(08-17)

    DIGITIMES reports AMD is looking at Taalas, the Canadian startup founded by former AMD and Nvidia architect Ljubisa Bajic that hard-wires models directly into silicon. If the approach scales it sidesteps HBM, advanced packaging and 3D stacking — precisely the bottlenecks now gating AI accelerator supply.

  10. [Tom's Hardware]Cherokee Nation bans hyperscale data centers across its reservation(08-17)

    The largest US tribe, with more than 475,000 citizens, barred hyperscale development on tribal and trust land after 64% of surveyed citizens objected over power, water, air quality and cultural resources. Existing projects on non-tribal land inside the 14-county reservation — including Google's $9 billion Pryor campus — are unaffected, and at least 69 US jurisdictions have now blocked new builds.
